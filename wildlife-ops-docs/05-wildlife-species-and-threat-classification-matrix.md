# Wildlife Species and Threat Classification Matrix

## 1. Purpose and limits of classification

This catalog defines operational handling for thirty named wildlife and domestic-animal categories relevant to North American households and selected global test scenarios. It is exhaustive for this thirty-entry release catalog, not for all wildlife species or jurisdictions. The model's task is visual identification; product policy combines that observation with trusted context and cautious homeowner language.

A screenshot cannot establish an animal's intent, disease status, ownership, exact distance, future path, or willingness to attack. A familiar species is not a safety clearance. A potentially dangerous species is not proof that an emergency is occurring. Preserve both principles when assigning priority and when evaluating model mistakes.

Scientific names are reference taxonomy, not a requirement that the model produce unsupported precision. Use a broader label such as bear, wild canine, large cat, or snake when the screenshot lacks diagnostic detail. Record uncertain presence separately from uncertain species.

## 2. Priority definitions

| Tier | Meaning | On-screen treatment | Escalation boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| T0 — Informational | A small or ordinary animal is plausibly identified without visible urgent context | Neutral animal observation with distance advice | Never display an all-clear statement |
| T1 — Caution | Interaction, size, pets, or poor visibility warrants extra separation | Clear caution with practical non-contact guidance | Homeowner reviews context without approaching |
| T2 — High caution | A potentially serious wildlife hazard or unresolved dangerous category is plausible | Prominent warning to remain separated and secure children and pets | Contact local wildlife or animal-control professionals when appropriate |
| T3 — Immediate danger | A person reports an active attack, injury, or immediate threat | Urgent, accessible emergency guidance | Human-verified context; not assigned solely from model species output |

The matrix provides default review tiers, not an automated emergency decision. Local rules, professional advice, actual barriers, and homeowner-reported circumstances can raise concern. Lowering an uncertain dangerous category to T0 because confidence is low is prohibited.

## 4. Evidence hierarchy and regional context

Use visible anatomy first, trusted camera-region context second, and model familiarity last. Regional context can identify implausible claims for review, but must not manufacture certainty. A global test clip intentionally showing a leopard must not be relabeled as a domestic cat merely because the test account uses a North American region.

Record the distinction between test-media origin and configured household region. The former supports dataset provenance; the latter influences product danger policy. Do not transmit precise household location merely to help a model choose among species. A coarse, consented region may be used only when the evaluation protocol requires it and privacy approval permits it.

## 5. Ambiguity, multiple animals, and uncertain categories

A coyote-versus-dog disagreement uses a cautious wild-canine or unknown-canine result, not a forced consensus. A mountain-lion-versus-cat disagreement remains unresolved when scale evidence is missing. Snake uncertainty retains non-contact advice regardless of whether the system can name a venomous species.

For multiple animals, evaluate each visible subject and present the highest justified caution without erasing the others. A clear squirrel does not cancel an obscured bear. Conversely, one uncertain shape must not create a confident claim that a pack of predators is present. Record count as uncertain when overlap prevents a reliable number.

Low confidence is not a risk-reduction mechanism. It limits what the product can claim about identity. The appropriate response can remain cautious while the species label becomes broader. Do not display aggressive, rabid, injured, pregnant, abandoned, or stray as image-derived facts without independently appropriate evidence.

## 6. Homeowner communication and escalation

Use [the exact approved alert texts](04-openrouter-system-prompts-and-response-templates.md). State that identification is an estimate, keep recommendations non-contact, and separate ordinary wildlife observation from an active emergency. Never recommend approaching an animal for a better screenshot, separating fighting animals, handling snakes, or using weapons.

If a homeowner reports immediate danger or injury, direct them to local emergency services and relevant professional advice. The workflow does not place calls, dispatch responders, or determine medical treatment. Local species guidance and emergency numbers must be reviewed by responsible regional specialists before production localization.

## 7. QA and maintenance requirements

Test all thirty entries as catalog coverage and prioritize supplied predator fixtures for held-out evaluation. Track category errors, overconfident species claims, dangerous false reassurance, and benign false alarms separately. A model that abstains appropriately may be preferable to one that invents precise taxonomy.

Review this catalog when adding a region, changing model families, or receiving a species-confusion defect. Preserve the previous catalog version and record why a tier changed. Publishing this document does not certify zoological completeness, prove model accuracy, or approve the application for safety-critical use.
