# Public Emergency Registries and Directory Data: Source Governance

**Status:** Desk-evaluation framework and proposed controls; source-specific availability, terms, and accuracy still require review. **Prepared:** 2026-11-11. **Accountable roles:** Data Steward, Regional Dispatch Liaison, Legal Reviewer, independent Quality Reviewer. No source listed here has been approved as a production contact feed by this change.

## 1. Evaluation objective and authority hierarchy

The directory needs agency identity, physical public-facing station locations, phone-number purpose, hours, service responsibility, and verification history. No single assumed national dataset should be treated as a complete, current, publicly callable emergency directory for every residential address. A public safety answering point, police headquarters, local precinct, administrative reception desk, and emergency dispatch function are different entities.

Use current information from the responsible local authority for contact publication, supported by independent confirmation for safety-critical fields. Federal catalogues and national geospatial datasets can help discover candidates and assess coverage, but their existence does not establish a usable emergency number or jurisdiction. Never infer phone-number quality from a government-looking name, search ranking, file format, or a record's latitude and longitude. Links in this handbook are research starting points, not evidence that every associated dataset exists today or permits redistribution.

## 2. Comparison matrix

| Source family and entry point | Useful coverage and fields | Authority and strengths | Gaps and restrictions to investigate | Proposed verification and fallback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Responsible municipal police and official city directories, starting from [region-specific government sites](02-nearest-police-directory-mapping.md) | Local station names, public addresses, published contact categories, hours | Best initial authority for its own published public contact information | Separate suburbs, sheriff contracts, after-hours limitations, and pages without update dates | Check monthly; independent agency confirmation quarterly; fall back to the agency's official administrative channel |
| County sheriff and county government directories | Unincorporated areas, county facilities, contract-service arrangements | Important where a city's postal name differs from police service responsibility | County boundaries do not necessarily equal service areas; jail and civil-process lines are not dispatch | Review monthly and after contract changes; consult the authorized county liaison when ambiguous |
| State or local 911 authority publications, discoverable through [911.gov](https://www.911.gov/) | Emergency communications governance, local guidance, possible service-area information | Relevant institutional context and routing coordination | Public access varies; not all operational service polygons or direct lines are publishable | Confirm publication permission and regional authority quarterly; use liaison confirmation without publishing restricted routing details |
| [FCC 911 Master PSAP Registry](https://www.fcc.gov/general/9-1-1-master-psap-registry) | PSAP identity and registry metadata where available | Federal registry reference useful for identity reconciliation | Not a residential-address routing oracle or a complete public telephone directory; review actual field definitions and current availability | Check the current release and metadata quarterly; resolve public contact questions with the responsible local authority |
| [Data.gov catalogue](https://catalog.data.gov/) | Discovery of datasets published by different agencies | Useful provenance trail to the actual publisher | Catalogue dates can describe metadata rather than record freshness; formats, licences, and coverage vary | Inspect each publisher's source and terms; do not treat a catalogue listing as independent verification |
| Official municipal or county open-data portals | Machine-readable police-facility points, public addresses, department inventories | Convenient structured data and documented publisher identity | May omit phone numbers, contain closed facilities, or lack operational hours and service boundaries | Compare source release monthly, confirm contacts quarterly, and quarantine records with unexplained schema or location changes |
| [Homeland Infrastructure Foundation-Level Data information](https://www.dhs.gov/homeland-infrastructure-foundation-level-data) and related government geospatial catalogues | Potential facility discovery and spatial comparison, subject to current access | Useful candidate inventory where public datasets are available | Dataset availability, access, sensitivity, redistribution, update frequency, and field completeness must be checked individually | Never assume historic datasets remain public or current; fall back to official local facility inventories |
| [Census TIGER/Line geography](https://www.census.gov/geographies/mapping-files/time-series/geo/tiger-line-file.html) | Municipal, county, and other geographic context | Documented geographic reference with published vintages | Census geography is not proof of police or PSAP jurisdiction and provides no validated station contact directory | Use the correct vintage for context only; confirm jurisdiction with the responsible authority |
| Commercial maps or community-maintained directories | Supplemental candidate discovery and apparent facility proximity | Can expose discrepancies worth investigation | Not government verification; licensing, stale numbers, duplication, and category errors are material risks | Never use as sole authority; reconcile every candidate against official evidence before display |

## 3. Field-level scoring and hard rejection rules

Score each proposed source from zero to three for publisher authority, geographic coverage, record freshness, semantic clarity, provenance, permitted reuse, and operational verification. Record both the score and the evidence supporting it. A high aggregate score cannot compensate for a failed safety field. Unclear number purpose, prohibited reuse, an unverified public address, unknown service responsibility, or no accountable maintainer must block operational publication of the affected record.

Keep source update dates separate from human verification dates and internal publication dates. A downloaded file timestamp does not prove when the department last checked a number. An unchanged website does not prove the contact still works. Record whether two references are actually independent; a municipal page and a third-party mirror of that page are one evidence chain, not two confirmations.

## 4. Provenance, licensing, and audit evidence

For each accepted record retain the canonical source URL, dataset or page title, publisher, version or retrieval date, licence or terms review, extracted field meanings, reviewer, verification method, and next review deadline. Keep a minimal redacted evidence snapshot or checksum where permitted. Do not store private liaison details, residential addresses, restricted PSAP routing data, or call recordings in public issues or Git history.

Public accessibility does not automatically mean public-domain status, an unrestricted scraping licence, or permission to republish a complete database. Legal must examine attribution, redistribution, automated-access restrictions, retention, and provider contracts. Respect robots and access controls; request authorized access rather than bypassing limitations. Document removed sources and the reason for removal so old exports are not silently reused.

## 5. Review calendar and change response

The proposed operational calendar includes monthly official-page checks, a quarterly documented liaison reconfirmation of active contacts, and a complete evidence check within 30 days before launch. For planning, active contact approval expires after 90 days unless a shorter local requirement or source restriction applies. These intervals are internal proposals requiring owner approval, not legal mandates or proof that any current record is valid.

A number-change report, closed station, broken authoritative page, agency merger, service-contract revision, jurisdiction disagreement, or suspected misuse triggers immediate quarantine and targeted review. Do not wait for the quarterly cycle. Track the affected region, last good release, severity, owner, fallback, and customer-facing limitation. A second reviewer must approve reactivation, with rollback or suppression procedures documented before the pilot.

## 6. Manual fallback and coverage gaps

When public evidence is incomplete, ask the authorized agency administrative contact to confirm service purpose, public location, hours, jurisdiction, and whether the contact may be republished. Use written confirmation or an approved scheduled administrative verification process. Never test by calling 911, sending emergency messages, or making fabricated incident reports. Record unanswered verification attempts and hold the listing rather than filling gaps with plausible numbers.

If an emergency is occurring, directory research must not delay advice to contact emergency services directly when safe. For routine unsupported locations, state that the directory cannot yet verify a local recommendation. Tribal, federal, transit, campus, rural, newly incorporated, and cross-border locations need explicit treatment; do not erase them by projecting municipal coverage over the entire metro. The five held planning regions remain excluded until equivalent evidence is approved.

## 7. Acceptance and ongoing accountability

A data-source audit is complete only when its reviewer can reproduce provenance, terms, field interpretation, freshness, dispute handling, and the approved fallback without accessing a real user's address. The Data Steward owns the register; the Regional Dispatch Liaison confirms local responsibility; Legal approves reuse; Quality independently checks safety fields. Missing evidence remains a tracked blocker, not an assumed pass.

Use the seven public-data-registry tasks to divide discovery, PSAP interpretation, municipal data quality, county and special jurisdictions, geography, reuse rights, and quarterly maintenance. Connect their outcomes to [the mapping workflow](02-nearest-police-directory-mapping.md), [privacy controls](04-privacy-and-address-compliance.md), and [the final readiness checklist](06-e2e-operational-readiness-checklist.md). Publishing this evaluation establishes an auditable plan; it does not constitute completed verification or endorsement of any data provider.
